About This Group

What We Are

The Human AI Consortium is a collaborative of independent practitioners at the intersection of Agile transformation, organizational design, change leadership, and AI adoption. We operate under a set of shared operating agreements, but have no formal legal structure — each member maintains their own practice and professional direction.

Our common background in helping teams build capability, leaders redesign systems, and organizations strengthen effectiveness and manage complex change brought us to a shared insight: that the organizations best positioned to realize the promise of AI are the ones willing to do the harder work of building the internal coherence to absorb it.

We came together in early 2026 around that belief — and around an even deeper conviction that work should be a source of meaning, connection, and genuine satisfaction. Organizations have a fundamental obligation to the people inside them — not merely to extract their labor but to create the conditions in which they can grow, contribute, and thrive. In a moment when AI is too often framed as a workforce reduction strategy, we are asking a different question: what does it look like when AI helps humans flourish?

Our purpose is to think through the AI transition together — and to sharpen our ability to meet what it demands of organizations, leaders, individuals, and the practitioners like us who help guide them.

Purpose

Why We Gather

We gather because we improve as practitioners when we think together — no single practitioner sees enough of the landscape to navigate it alone. This group is a place to pressure-test ideas, share knowledge and opportunities, deepen our expertise at the intersection of AI and organizational transformation, and build collective credibility in a domain evolving faster than any one of us can track.

When it makes sense, we work together on specific projects.

We have identified a genuine gap: the human and organizational layer of AI transformation remains largely underserved by practitioners who understand it the way we do.

Core Beliefs

What We Believe

These are the working principles that anchor our thinking. They guide what we pay attention to, how we approach client work, and how we understand the work of organizational transformation in the age of AI.

01

The right human strategy is the right business strategy.

The way an organization treats its people and designs the environment in which they work is not a separate conversation from its business strategy — it is the business strategy. Trust, decision clarity, genuine accountability, and a culture of continuous learning are not nice-to-haves. They are economic multipliers. Organizations that invest meaningfully in their people will outperform those that don't.

02

Unlocked human capacity is the right measure.

The economic return on AI is found in what becomes possible when people are freed from cognitive labor that machines can increasingly handle. But freed capacity only generates value when it is redirected toward work of higher value. The measure of success is human potential realized, not human capital eliminated.

03

Organizational intelligence is the force multiplier.

Every investment an organization makes — in technology, talent, or strategy — is only as good as the system it runs through. The return on AI investment is directly proportional to the organization's existing level of coherence. AI amplifies what already exists.

04

Systems shape behavior. Behavior shapes outcomes.

When results are disappointing, the instinct is to look at the people. But people operate inside structures that leaders build. An organization that isn't getting the outcomes it wants is usually getting exactly what its system was designed to produce. The work is not to fix the people. The work is to examine the system.

05

Leaders must change first.

In high-complexity environments, centralized decision-making is a structural constraint. Sustained performance requires cultures where people at every level can exercise good judgment, guided by clear vision, shared principles, and genuine trust. But those cultures start at the top. Leaders who ask their organizations to change without changing how they themselves lead will consistently underdeliver on what they set out to build.

06

Diagnose before you design.

Organizations under pressure to transform often skip the foundational work: an honest assessment of where the organization actually is, a clear vision of where it is headed, and the willingness to acknowledge the gap. Without that, transformation becomes a series of reactions — busy, expensive, and unlikely to stick. You cannot design your way out of a problem you cannot see clearly.

07

Capability must precede velocity.

You cannot move faster than the organization's capacity to sustain what you're building. The real speed of any transition is determined by how quickly people can genuinely build understanding, develop new skills, and absorb those changes — not how quickly a training program can be rolled out or a tool deployed. Organizations that skip this in favor of urgency find themselves perpetually reacting, never actually moving fast.

Operating Norms

How We Work Together

These principles govern the intent behind how we operate — as peers and as a collective.

We operate as peers. There is no hierarchy here. Members bring different expertise and different professional contexts. All of it has value.
We share in good faith. We operate with transparency about our intentions and generosity about our knowledge.
We show up actively. Passive membership isn't what this is for. The value of this group is what members put into it, not just what they take from it.
We hold high professional standards. In how we represent our expertise, how we engage with clients, and how we represent the group externally.
We practice what we preach. We advocate for human-centered, disciplined transformation in client organizations. We hold ourselves to the same standard — in how we make decisions, handle disagreements, and treat each other.
We value intellectual honesty. Our credibility depends on our willingness to do the hard work, say the hard things, and resist the shortcuts. We do not chase hype, overpromise what our guidance can deliver, or claim certainty where the honest answer is that we're still learning.

Our methodology — Structured Disruption — reflects these same principles in client work: deliberate, diagnostically grounded, and designed to build lasting capability rather than the illusion of progress.
